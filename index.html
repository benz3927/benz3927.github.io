Working paper Submitted to JMCB Jan 2026 – Aug 2026 SSRN 7326939

Dissent, Deliberation, and Regional Influence at FOMC Meetings

with Ann L. Owen (Hamilton College, former Federal Reserve economist). Co-first author; I led the measurement pipeline, the econometrics, and the final round of revisions. Grew out of my honors thesis; awarded the Soper Research Prize.

Formal FOMC votes almost never dissent, so the votes hide most of the disagreement in the room. We read the transcripts instead. A multi-model LLM pipeline (Claude and GPT-4o) scores every participant's economic assessment and policy preference at every meeting from 1990 to 2020, giving a stance measure for the seven non-voting Reserve Bank presidents as well as the voters.

Two findings. Regional economic conditions shape how presidents talk about policy, not just how they vote. And non-voters matter: their discussion is associated with voters revising their views within the meeting, with recorded dissents, and with the position the Chair carries into the next meeting's proposal.

Before running any regression I treated the measurement as its own problem. Early scoring versions were not accurate enough to trust, so the pipeline was rebuilt and validated against two independent benchmarks before touching the panel.

Data
Roughly 30 years of FOMC transcripts, 1990 to 2020, every participant and every meeting
Validation
100% directional agreement with the Thornton-Wheelock dissent record; 100% agreement with Meade (2005) hand-coded stances
Econometrics
Panel regressions with Driscoll-Kraay standard errors, implemented in Python and checked against Stata (see xtscc)
Stack
Python, R, Stata, Claude and OpenAI APIs

DProQV2: protein structure quality assessment with a gated graph transformer

Research assistant, Hamilton College Computer Science.

A 12-layer gated graph transformer that predicts per-residue lDDT for protein decoys, combining ESM-C sequence embeddings with Rosetta energy terms as node and edge features. Trained on more than a million decoy structures on Hamilton's HPC cluster (multi-GPU, NVIDIA A100) and submitted to the CASP17 benchmark.

My part was the experiment design and the training infrastructure: data loaders that did not choke on a million graphs, checkpointing, and keeping runs reproducible across nodes.

Model
12-layer gated graph transformer, rotation-invariant features
Scale
1M+ decoys, multi-GPU training
Stack
PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, Slurm, ESM-C, Rosetta
